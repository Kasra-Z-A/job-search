Finding jobs by asking for them

You describe the job you want in your own words. The assistant works out what you mean, finds matching postings among 9,458 remote jobs, and says which fit best and why. Every step that can be measured is measured on 1,464 test searches with known answers.

The system

From a message to an answer

Two parts. Once per snapshot, the postings are crawled, cleaned and embedded into an index. Then every message runs three steps against that index: understand it, retrieve candidates, answer. The brackets show which steps the evaluation below measures.

Measured, not eyeballed

Judging search results usually means reading them by hand. Here the answers are known in advance. The test searches are built from the jobs' own fields, for example senior · Developer · open to Canada · at least $140k, rewritten into everyday language by one LLM and checked against their fields by another. Each system is scored on the same searches by the share of its top ten results that meet everything the search asked for.

What the 99% means

The search filters on the same fields the test answers come from. Once it understands a request, everything it returns qualifies, so the score mostly measures understanding: 98% of test searches are read exactly right. Most misses are "management role", which the job board uses both as a job family and as a level. What this test cannot see is how well a job fits the topic inside a broad family such as "Developer"; that judgement is what the answer step adds. So the claim is "it applies what you asked for", not "every result is perfect".

Without the structured fields

Many job sites only have the posting text. In that setting an LLM reads every description and records what it states, leaving the rest unknown. That more than doubles precision over meaning-based search, but stays far below the version with structured fields, and the reason is missing information: salary appears in 85% of descriptions that have one, location in about half, contract type in a third.

Checks and limits

  • Forty deliberately sloppy searches ("mid dev job philipines fulltime") score 0.997, against 1.000 for the same searches worded cleanly.
  • The test searches were written by an LLM, and the assistant reads them with one. A comparison with searches written by people is still open.
  • The scores measure the first message. Follow-ups and the written answers are not part of the evaluation yet.
  • The job board's own tags are not perfect: a hand check of 48 seniority tags agreed with 88%.
  • Differences between systems come with 95% confidence intervals over searches; keywords plus meaning is not reliably better than meaning alone.
  • An answer costs about $0.002 in API calls. Building and evaluating everything cost about $5.
